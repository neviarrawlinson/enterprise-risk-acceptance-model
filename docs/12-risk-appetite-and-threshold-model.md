# Risk Appetite & Threshold Model

Risk acceptance decisions must align with the organization’s defined risk appetite.

Risk appetite defines the amount and type of risk the organization is willing to accept in pursuit of strategic objectives.

---

## 1. Risk Appetite Principles

- No appetite for regulatory violations.
- Low appetite for risks involving sensitive customer data.
- Moderate appetite for temporary operational risk.
- Higher tolerance for short-term tactical constraints with defined remediation.

---

## 2. Residual Risk Threshold Alignment

Residual Risk Score → Action

1–6  
Operationally acceptable with standard documentation.

7–14  
Acceptable with business owner sign-off and remediation plan.

15–25  
Requires executive visibility and formal acceptance.

---

## 3. Automatic Escalation Triggers

Regardless of numeric score, escalation is required when:

- Customer PII exposure potential exists
- Regulatory reporting thresholds may be triggered
- Risk spans multiple legal entities
- Risk renewal occurs more than once
- Active exploitation context is present

---

## 4. Alignment to Enterprise Objectives

Risk appetite must support:

- Revenue continuity
- Regulatory compliance
- Brand protection
- Customer trust
- Operational resilience

---

## 5. Review Cadence

Risk appetite should be reviewed:

- Annually at minimum
- After material incidents
- Following regulatory changes
- When entering new markets

---

## Guiding Principle

Risk acceptance is not risk avoidance.

It is a conscious, documented decision aligned with leadership-defined tolerance.
