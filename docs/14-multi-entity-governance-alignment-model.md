# Multi-Entity Governance Alignment Model

Large enterprises with multiple legal entities require a governance model that balances central oversight with local operational flexibility.

This model supports unified standards without imposing unnecessary control burdens on smaller entities.

---

## 1. Governance Structure Overview

Central Security Function:
- Defines risk framework
- Maintains risk scoring methodology
- Owns risk appetite thresholds
- Operates centralized risk register
- Oversees Tier 3 risk acceptance

Business Unit / Entity Level:
- Owns operational risk decisions
- Submits risk acceptance requests
- Implements compensating controls
- Maintains accountability for remediation

Governance is centralized.
Execution is decentralized.

---

## 2. Entity Classification Model

Entities may be categorized based on:

- Revenue size
- Data sensitivity
- Regulatory exposure
- Technology footprint
- External connectivity

Example Classification:

Tier A – Major revenue-generating entities  
Tier B – Mid-size operational units  
Tier C – Small management LLCs / low-technology footprint entities  

Governance requirements scale accordingly.

---

## 3. Proportional Governance Approach

Tier A Entities:
- Full risk scoring required
- Formal Tier routing
- Quarterly review participation
- SLA enforcement

Tier B Entities:
- Standardized intake process
- Tier routing where applicable
- Semi-annual review

Tier C Entities:
- Lightweight risk intake
- Delegated approval authority
- Annual oversight validation

Small entities should not be governed like regulated financial institutions.

---

## 4. Partial Ownership Considerations

Where ownership is less than 100%:

- Define responsibility boundaries clearly
- Identify contractual security obligations
- Clarify reporting and escalation pathways
- Document shared risk accountability

Ownership percentage does not eliminate reputational exposure.

---

## 5. Centralized Reporting Model

All entities feed into a centralized reporting dashboard.

Leadership receives:

- Aggregated Tier 3 exposure
- Risk distribution by entity class
- Expiration hygiene metrics
- Thematic trend analysis

This preserves visibility without centralizing daily operations.

---

## 6. Guiding Principle

The objective is consistency, not uniformity.

Standards must be shared.
Implementation may vary based on entity size and risk profile.
