# Risk Acceptance Standard

## 1. Policy Statement

Risk acceptance is the formal decision to tolerate residual risk when remediation is not feasible, not cost-effective, or not aligned with business priorities.

Risk acceptance transfers accountability to a defined business owner. It does not eliminate risk, and it does not replace remediation planning.

All accepted risks must be documented, approved at the appropriate authority level, and time-bound.

---

## 2. Guiding Principles

- Risk must be explicitly owned
- Acceptance must be time-bound
- Compensating controls should be applied where possible
- Decisions must be auditable
- Renewals require re-evaluation

---

## 3. Required Components of Every Risk Acceptance

Every risk acceptance record must include:

- Clear risk description and scope
- Inherent and residual risk rating
- Business impact statement (in plain language)
- Named business risk owner
- Compensating controls (if applicable)
- Defined expiration date
- Remediation plan or justification for non-remediation
- Approval signatures based on tier thresholds
- Review cadence

---

## 4. Prohibited Uses

Risk acceptance must not be used to:

- Bypass legal or regulatory obligations
- Ignore active exploitation scenarios
- Repeatedly delay remediation without justification
- Avoid security standards for convenience

---

## 5. Expiration and Renewal

All risk acceptances must expire.

Renewal requires:

- Updated risk assessment
- Validation of compensating controls
- Confirmation from risk owner
- Re-approval if material conditions changed
